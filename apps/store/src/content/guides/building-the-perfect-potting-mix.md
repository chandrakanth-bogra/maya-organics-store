The biggest mistake container gardeners make is reaching for off-the-shelf potting soil and calling it done. Commercial mixes compact over time, retain too much moisture, and often lack the aeration that roots actually need. Building your own mix takes minutes and pays dividends for the life of your plants.

## The three components of a great mix

A good potting mix balances three things: structure (what holds the mix together), drainage (what moves water through), and nutrition (what feeds the plant).

- Structure: Cocopeat or a quality potting base provides moisture retention and a root-friendly texture.
- Drainage & aeration: Perlite, pumice, or LECA break compaction and create air pockets around roots.
- Nutrition: Vermicompost or neem cake add slow-release nutrients and beneficial microbes.

## A general-purpose mix (works for most houseplants)

Start with this ratio and adjust based on your plant's needs:

- 50% cocopeat
- 30% perlite
- 20% vermicompost

## For succulents and cacti

Succulents need fast drainage above everything else. Increase the inorganic fraction significantly:

- 30% cocopeat
- 40% pumice or perlite
- 20% coarse sand or grit
- 10% vermicompost

## For orchids

Orchids need extreme aeration and almost no moisture retention. LECA works well as a standalone medium for semi-hydro setups, or use a chunky bark-and-perlite mix.

## Final tips

Pre-wet your cocopeat before mixing. Dry coir is hydrophobic and won't absorb water evenly. Soak it, break it apart, then combine with your amendments. And always choose horticultural-grade perlite — construction-grade has different particle sizes and may contain impurities.
