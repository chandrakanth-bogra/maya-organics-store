Neem cake is pressed neem seed residue — what remains after oil extraction. Cold-pressed neem cake retains the full spectrum of neem's bioactive limonoid compounds including azadirachtin, the primary pest-fighting molecule. It works as a slow-release NPK fertiliser and as a soil defender simultaneously. This guide covers everything you need to know to use it correctly.

## Cold-Pressed vs Solvent-Extracted

This distinction is the single most important thing to know about neem cake. Most cheap neem cake on the market is solvent-extracted.

- **Cold-pressed neem cake:** 800–2,000+ ppm azadirachtin. Darker colour, oily sheen, strong pungent smell. Leaves an oil stain when pressed on paper. Retains 8–15% residual oil.
- **Solvent-extracted neem cake:** 50–300 ppm azadirachtin. The chemical extraction process destroys most bioactive compounds. Lighter colour, dry texture, weak smell. Effective mainly as a fertiliser, not a pest defender.

How to identify cold-pressed at purchase: darker brown colour, strong and pungent neem smell, slightly oily feel, leaves a mark on paper when pressed. Our neem cake is cold-pressed only.

## How Neem Defends Your Soil

Azadirachtin and related limonoid compounds work through seven distinct mechanisms — making them far harder for pests to develop resistance to than single-target pesticides:

- **Antifeedant** — insects exposed to azadirachtin stop feeding. They do not die immediately but starve.
- **Insect growth regulator** — disrupts moulting (ecdysone hormone interference). Larvae cannot develop into adults.
- **Repellent** — insects avoid soil and plant surfaces treated with neem compounds.
- **Oviposition deterrent** — prevents egg-laying on treated plants and in treated soil.
- **Nematicide** — inhibits root-knot nematode (Meloidogyne spp.) development. Particularly effective in container vegetable growing in South India.
- **Antifungal** — suppresses soil-borne fungal pathogens.
- **Systemic action** — plant roots absorb azadirachtin and distribute it through leaf tissue, protecting above-ground parts.

Key pests controlled: root-knot nematodes, white grubs (Holotrichia spp.), fungus gnats, termites, aphids, mealybugs, whiteflies, thrips.

## How Neem Feeds Your Plants

- **NPK profile:** Approximately 4:1:2 — higher nitrogen than most organic amendments
- **Nitrification inhibitor:** Neem cake naturally inhibits Nitrosomonas bacteria from converting ammonium to leachable nitrate. Nitrogen stays in the root zone longer — especially valuable during Telangana's monsoon when heavy rains flush nutrients from pots.
- **Trace minerals:** Sulphur, calcium, magnesium, and micronutrients present in the residual seed matter
- **Organic matter:** Improves soil structure and microbial activity over time

## Dosage by Pot Size

| Pot Size | Amount | Frequency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4–6 inch | ½ – 1 tsp (2–5g) | Every 4–6 weeks |
| 8–10 inch | 1–2 tbsp (10–20g) | Every 4–6 weeks |
| 12 inch | 2–4 tbsp (20–50g) | Every 4–6 weeks |
| 16 inch+ | 50–100g | Every 4–6 weeks |

At mixing time: add 5% neem cake by volume to potting mix. This gives background protection from the moment of planting.

## Important Rules

⚠️ **Never add neem cake to seed-starting mixes.** Bioactive compounds can suppress delicate seedling roots. Wait until plants have at least 2–3 true leaves before first application.

⚠️ **Container plants need half the dose recommended for garden beds.** The concentrated root zone amplifies effects — what is safe in open ground can be too strong in a pot.

⚠️ **Allow 3–4 weeks before harvesting edible crops after application.** Signs of overdose: leaf tip burn, excessively dark green foliage, stunted roots.

## Neem Cake Tea Recipe

A liquid drench delivers azadirachtin directly to the root zone and works faster than granular application for active pest infestations.

1. Add 1kg neem cake to 10 litres of water in a bucket.
2. Stir thoroughly to mix.
3. Let steep for 24–48 hours at room temperature, stirring periodically.
4. Strain out the solid cake through cloth or a fine sieve.
5. Dilute 1 part neem tea with 10 parts water (1:10 ratio).
6. Apply immediately as a soil drench. Do not store — use within a few hours of dilution.
7. Apply in the evening to prevent rapid UV breakdown of azadirachtin.
