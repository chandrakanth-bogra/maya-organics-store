Vermicompost is organic matter that has been broken down by earthworms. What comes out the other end is something remarkable: a crumbly, dark, odourless material packed with plant-available nutrients, beneficial bacteria, growth hormones, and enzymes that dramatically improve soil life.

## What's actually in vermicompost

- NPK: Nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium in plant-available form — released slowly over time
- Micronutrients: Calcium, magnesium, sulphur, zinc, and more
- Beneficial bacteria and fungi: Improve nutrient uptake and protect roots from pathogens
- Humic acids: Improve soil structure and nutrient chelation
- Plant growth hormones: Auxins and cytokinins that directly stimulate root and shoot growth

## How to use vermicompost

- In potting mixes: 10–20% by volume as a slow-release nutritional component
- As a top dressing: Apply 1–2cm around the base of established plants and water in
- As a liquid feed: Steep 1 part vermicompost in 5 parts water for 24 hours, strain, and apply
- For seed starting: Mix 10–15% into seedling mix for gentle nutrition

## Quality signals to look for

Good vermicompost is dark brown to black, earthy-smelling (like fresh soil, not compost), and crumbly — not wet or slimy. It should have no visible unprocessed organic matter. If it smells sour or has large chunks of undecomposed material, it hasn't finished processing.

## Vermicompost vs chemical fertiliser

Chemical fertilisers deliver nutrients quickly but do nothing for soil life. Repeated use of chemical-only feeding degrades soil biology over time. Vermicompost feeds the soil food web, which in turn feeds your plant — the way nature intended. Use it as your base nutrition and supplement with liquid fertilisers if needed.
