LECA stands for Lightweight Expanded Clay Aggregate — small, porous clay balls that have been fired at very high temperatures until they expand. They don't compress, don't degrade, and can be washed and reused indefinitely. For a certain type of grower, LECA is life-changing.

## How LECA works

Plants grown in LECA are typically set up in a 'semi-hydro' system: the pot sits in a reservoir of nutrient solution, and the LECA wicks moisture up to the roots through capillary action. The roots at the bottom stay hydrated; the roots at the top stay in air. It's almost impossible to overwater.

## What you need to get started

- LECA balls (rinsed thoroughly before first use)
- A nursery pot with drainage holes
- An outer pot or tray to hold the reservoir
- A hydroponic nutrient solution (liquid fertiliser designed for hydroponics)
- A way to check EC and pH (optional but recommended)

## The reservoir method step by step

1. Rinse LECA in clean water for several minutes to remove dust.
2. Fill your inner pot with LECA and place your plant, spreading roots through the medium.
3. Set the inner pot inside an outer pot or tray.
4. Add nutrient solution to the outer pot so the bottom 1–2cm of the inner pot sits in liquid.
5. Let the solution drop to near-zero before refilling — this creates a wet-dry cycle that strengthens roots.

## Transitioning from soil to LECA

Wash all the soil off the roots completely before moving to LECA. Soil particles left behind will rot and introduce bacteria. Soak the bare roots in clean water for a few days before planting in LECA to help them adjust to the new environment.

## Best plants for LECA

Monsteras, pothos, philodendrons, peace lilies, orchids, and most tropical foliage plants thrive in LECA. Succulents and cacti are not ideal — they prefer dry periods that the semi-hydro method doesn't provide well.
