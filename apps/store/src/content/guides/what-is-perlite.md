If you've ever bought potting soil, you've likely noticed small white beads that look like polystyrene. That isn't foam — it's **perlite**, a crucial component for healthy plant growth.

## The Origins of Perlite

Perlite is a naturally occurring volcanic glass. When heated to around 870–900°C, it expands 4 to 20 times its original volume. This makes it incredibly lightweight and gives it a porous, open structure that is highly valuable in horticulture.

## Why Use Perlite?

- **Drainage:** Its porous structure allows excess water to drain away freely, preventing root rot.
- **Aeration:** It creates air pockets in the soil, giving roots the oxygen they need to thrive.
- **Sterile:** Being a heat-processed mineral, perlite carries no pathogens, fungus or weed seeds.
- **pH neutral:** It won't alter the chemistry of your soil mix.
- **Reusable:** Rinse and reuse it season after season.

## How to Use It

A standard mix for most houseplants is **70% coco peat + 30% perlite**. For succulents and cacti, increase perlite to 50–60% for faster drainage. For propagation and seedlings, use a 50/50 blend of perlite and vermiculite.
