When people overwater, the water is rarely the actual problem. The problem is soil that holds water too long and starves roots of oxygen. Roots need both water and air — and when a pot stays saturated for days, the air pockets collapse, beneficial bacteria die, and anaerobic conditions invite root rot.

## How drainage works in a container

In a pot, water has nowhere to go except through the drainage hole — or into the air through evaporation. Dense soil slows this process dramatically. A mix with adequate perlite or pumice creates channels that allow water to drain in minutes, not days.

## Signs your mix lacks drainage

- Soil stays wet for more than 4–5 days after watering
- You see yellowing lower leaves despite regular feeding
- A musty smell from the soil
- Wilting even when the soil is wet (classic root rot symptom)

## The fix: increase your inorganic fraction

The fastest way to improve drainage is to repot with more perlite, pumice, or LECA in the mix. For most houseplants, 25–40% perlite by volume is the sweet spot. For succulents and cacti, go higher — 40–60%.

## Zeolite as a drainage aid

Zeolite is underrated for drainage. It improves soil porosity while also capturing and slowly releasing nutrients and ammonium. Adding 10–15% zeolite to a mix improves both drainage and nutrient retention simultaneously.
