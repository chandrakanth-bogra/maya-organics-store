Zeolite is a naturally occurring mineral with a highly porous, cage-like crystal structure. These tiny pores give zeolite an enormous surface area — one gram of zeolite has roughly the surface area of a football field. This structure allows it to capture, store, and slowly release nutrients and water in a way few other minerals can.

## What zeolite does in soil

- Cation exchange: Zeolite captures ammonium (NH4+) and other positively charged nutrients, preventing them from washing out with irrigation
- Slow-release nutrition: Captured nutrients are released gradually as plants need them
- Drainage improvement: The porous structure increases soil aeration without sacrificing too much moisture
- pH buffering: Zeolite has mild pH-buffering properties that help maintain a stable root zone

## How to use zeolite

- In potting mixes: Add 10–20% by volume as a partial replacement for perlite
- In garden beds: Broadcast 100–200g per square metre and till into the top 15cm
- For lawn care: Apply 50g per square metre to improve drainage in compacted lawns
- In aquariums and hydroponics: Zeolite is safe and effective for capturing ammonia in water

## Zeolite vs perlite

Perlite is primarily for drainage and aeration — it has almost no nutrient value and doesn't improve water quality. Zeolite does less for drainage than perlite, but adds significant nutritional benefits. The best approach is often to use both together: perlite for drainage, zeolite for nutrient retention.

## Recharging zeolite

Over time, zeolite's cation exchange capacity can become saturated. You can 'recharge' it by soaking in a dilute solution of calcium nitrate or potassium solution, which displaces the captured ammonium and refills the exchange sites. This makes zeolite a long-term investment in your soil.
